Figures illustrate the differences in the market capitalization of firms listed on the S&P 1,500 Composite Index, within and across different sectors.

The scaling exponent (α) quantifies concentration risk for all firms at or above given market caps, or cut-off points, denoted by the blue vertical lines. The heavy tails of the distributions suggest that there is significant concentration risk across all sectors, although the risk profiles vary substantially.

Smaller α identify sectors with greater levels of risk concentration, where the largest of the large-caps dominate the sector. Tech firms, for instance, exhibit the smallest scaling exponent (α = 1.79). The tech industry, therefore, is the most risky given the egregious levels of concentration in tech. That is, it is dominated by small numbers of large firms (e.g., Apple, Microsoft, NVIDIA).

Larger α identify sectors with less risk concentration, comparatively, such that there is more uniformity in the market caps of firms in those sectors. The real estate sector, for instance, exhibits the largest scaling exponent (α = 3.11). The size of this exponent (α) suggests that, unlike the tech industry, small numbers of large-cap firms do not dominate this sector. Instead, this sector more so consists of small- and mid-cap firms.

The analysis suggests that large-caps dominate small-α sectors (e.g., Tech) and weighted indices (e.g., S&P 500), regardless of portfolio construction or diversification of the indices. Investments in mid- and small-cap indices (S&P 400 and S&P 600), and the firms that comprise those indices, offer greater diversification in portfolio construction, and less risk.
